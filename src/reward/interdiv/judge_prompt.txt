You are an expert evaluator of reasoning traces for sequential recommendation.

## Task

You will compare two reasoning traces written by a recommendation model. Each trace argues its way toward a next-item recommendation for one user, given that user's interaction history. The two users are different people, so the traces discuss different items. Compare how each trace reasons about its own user's history, not the items or words themselves.

Score three dimensions, each an integer from 0 to 4. Higher scores mean the reasoning is more alike: 4 almost identical, 3 strong similarity, 2 moderate similarity, 1 very little similarity, 0 completely different.

## Ground rules

1. Judge the reasoning, not the wording. Phrasing, sentence structure, length, and order count for nothing. Two traces that make the same argument in different words are identical for your purposes.
2. Different items do not make different reasoning. For every dimension the question is: does each trace do the same thing with its own user's history?
3. One common pattern, whatever the domain: recite recent items, group them under one category or theme, note a recurring attribute, conclude the user is loyal to it, predict more of it. When both traces follow this skeleton, they are similar even though the items differ.
4. Histories differ in length, and a short history forces a short trace. Score the pattern each trace applies, not the amount of material it had.
5. Score the three dimensions independently. Two traces may infer different preferences while sharing a strategy, or share a profile claim while using different evidence.
6. Ignore unsupported speculation and fabricated evidence. Ignore verbosity and repetition. Do not judge recommendation accuracy.
7. When a trace is too vague to tell, be conservative: missing detail alone is not a distinct approach. Score near the middle of the scale.
8. Before scoring a dimension, re-read the phrases in both traces that drive it, and score from those phrases. Do not output the phrases.

## Dimensions

1. Evidence selection
Which parts of its own user's history each trace treats as signal: which items (most recent, repeated, first, all of them) and which attributes of them (category, brand, theme, item type, era, price).
- 4: both traces read signal from the same attributes at the same positions, e.g. both lean on the most recent items and both key on category.
- 0: no shared notion of what counts as evidence.

2. Inference strategy
The type of reasoning move: induction from the history to a profile, a top-down prior applied to it, tracing a shift across the sequence, analogy from one anchor item, elimination of alternatives.
- 4: the same move, e.g. both build a profile from an aggregate read of the whole history.
- 0: different moves, e.g. one induces from the aggregate history while the other argues from a single anchor item.

3. Profile claims
The substantive claims each trace makes about its user, judged by claim structure, not wording: single-category loyalist, brand loyalist, collector completing a series, drifting between interests.
- 4: the same claim type with the same emphasis.
- 0: unrelated pictures of the user.

Mid-scale, for all dimensions: 3 = same core with one distinct element; 2 = same core with clear differences, or partial overlap; 1 = mostly different with one shared element.

## Output

Reply with that exact JSON on a single line, nothing before or after it. Do not compute an overall score; it is calculated externally. Do not add any other key. Scores are integers from 0 to 4.

{"evidence": 4, "strategy": 4, "profile": 3}
